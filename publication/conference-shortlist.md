## Score, acceptance-rate, and ICORE interpretation

- **Fit score** is a manuscript-specific assessment, not a conference metric.
  It combines topical alignment, methodological alignment, expected audience,
  paper-length compatibility, and how much reframing the current manuscript
  would require.
- **Published acceptance rate** is shown only when the conference itself
  provides the figure. An em dash means that no reliable published rate was
  found; no estimated rates are used.
- Computing Conference publishes a claimed historical acceptance rate of
  37–39% on its official call for papers.
- BPOD does not publish submission totals. Its parent IEEE Big Data conference
  reports recent main-track rates around 18%, but that rate must not be applied
  to BPOD because workshops conduct their own selection. BPOD's historical
  pages report accepted-paper counts only.
- **ICORE rank** was checked in every source/year available through the
  official [ICORE Conference Portal](https://portal.core.edu.au/conf-ranks/),
  including historical CORE and ERA sources—not only ICORE 2026. “Not listed”
  means that no matching venue entry was found in any source; it is different
  from an explicit portal rank of “Unranked.” BPOD itself is not listed and
  does not inherit the **B** rank held by its parent IEEE Big Data conference
  in the current and recent CORE/ICORE sources.

## Ranking rationale

1. **BPOD 2026** is the closest match because it explicitly covers large
   models, automatic performance tuning, workflow optimization, HPC/cloud
   environments, and sustainability.
2. **SMARTGREENS 2027** directly covers energy-aware systems, sustainable
   computing, Green ICT, and energy engineering. This is an excellent match
   for the carbon-aware scheduling and simulation contributions.
3. **3SCEA 2027** explicitly welcomes low-carbon AI, optimization for
   sustainability, resource optimization, and sustainability-oriented
   simulation and performance evaluation.
4. **IEEA 2027** strongly matches carbon-aware computing through its
   environmental and energy informatics scope, although it is less focused on
   LLM systems optimization.
5. **Computing Conference 2027** includes LLMs, scalable systems, green
   computing, and sustainable AI, but it is a broader computing venue.
6. **AIPE 2027** covers LLMs, decision intelligence, scheduling, and energy,
   but its central audience is power engineering rather than computing-system
   optimization.
7. **ICGHIT 2027** covers green computing, energy saving, AI, and system
   architecture. Its six-page maximum would require substantial shortening.
8. **ICSISCET 2026** broadly covers sustainable engineering and computing, but
   its scope is less specific and its submission deadline is much closer.
9. **GECOST 2027** has relevant green-computing and AI tracks, but its scope is
   broad, its deadline is close, and IEEE Xplore submission is conditional.

## Publication notes

- BPOD states that accepted papers will appear in the IEEE Big Data 2026
  proceedings published by the IEEE Computer Society.
- IEEA states that accepted and registered papers can be published in ACM
  conference proceedings.
- SMARTGREENS publishes presented papers in the SCITEPRESS Digital Library.
  Springer CCIS applies only to a selected set of revised papers and is listed
  as pending final approval.
- 3SCEA is partially sponsored by the IEEE Egypt Section and provides a live
  Microsoft CMT submission portal; final IEEE publication remains subject to
  the publisher's requirements.
- Computing Conference states that accepted papers are published by Springer
  in the *Lecture Notes in Networks and Systems* series.
- AIPE describes peer-reviewed conference proceedings and lists its previous
  editions in IEEE Xplore. “Planned” is used above because final inclusion in a
  publisher's digital library normally depends on publication review and
  compliance.
- ICGHIT states that accepted and presented papers are submitted to IEEE
  Xplore, with no guarantee of inclusion after IEEE's additional screening.
- ICSISCET states that accepted papers will be published in Springer's
  *Lecture Notes in Electrical Engineering* series.
- GECOST says papers are “potentially” submitted to IEEE Xplore, so IEEE
  publication should not be treated as guaranteed.
